data modeling I’ll outline a set of algorithms and modeling patterns you can use in Icarus to turn fragmented data into **priors** and **statistical probabilities** about deterioration, system failure, and risk—without reducing the person to a diagnosis or letting any single institution define the narrative.

This is framed for your stack (Python + Supabase + AI orchestration) and for the Lindsay-style case as a template.

***

## 1) Modeling Goals (in Probabilistic Terms)

You’re trying to estimate, over time, quantities like:

- \( P(\text{severe deterioration}_t \mid \text{history}_{0:t}) \)
- \( P(\text{system missed handoff}_t \mid \text{contacts}_{0:t}, \text{records}_{0:t}) \)
- \( P(\text{unrecognized crisis}_t \mid \text{signals}_{0:t}, \text{care structure}_t) \)

“Data as priors” means:

- Use historical cases (Lindsay + other postpartum cases) to form **base-rate priors**.
- Update those priors with **case-specific evidence** (her journal, meds, sleep, clinical contacts).
- Keep uncertainty explicit and auditable.

***

## 2) Core Probabilistic Frameworks

### 2.1 Hierarchical Bayesian Models (HBMs)

Use HBMs to encode:

- **Population-level priors**: typical postpartum trajectories for sleep, mood, suicidality, treatment response.
- **Case-level parameters**: Lindsay’s specific trajectory, deviations from population norms.
- **System-level parameters**: typical fragmentation rates, handoff failure rates, follow-up adherence.

Structure:

- Level 1 (population):  
  \[
  \theta_{\text{pop}} \sim \text{Prior}(\cdot)
  \]
- Level 2 (case-specific):  
  \[
  \theta_{\text{Lindsay}} \sim \text{Distribution}(\theta_{\text{pop}})
  \]
- Level 3 (observations):  
  \[
  y_t \sim \text{Likelihood}(\theta_{\text{Lindsay}}, \text{context}_t)
  \]

Algorithms / tools:

- **MCMC** (PyMC, Stan) for full posterior inference when data is modest but rich.
- **Variational inference** (PyMC, TensorFlow Probability) for faster approximate posteriors.
- Use these to estimate:
  - Probability she was in a “high-risk” latent state at each time slice.
  - Probability the system was in a “fragmented care” state.

Output:

- Time-indexed posterior distributions, not just point estimates.
- Clear priors derived from aggregate data; posteriors updated by her specific record.

***

### 2.2 State-Space Models / Hidden Markov Models (HMMs)

Model her **latent mental/physiological state** over time as hidden states, with observed data as emissions.

States might include:

- \( S_t \in \{\text{stable}, \text{distressed}, \text{crisis}, \text{post-crisis}\} \)
- Or finer-grained: sleep-deprived, medicated, suicidal, psychotic, etc.

Observations:

- Journal sentiment and content tags.
- Sleep duration/quality.
- Med changes.
- Clinical contacts.
- Observed behavior from others.

Model:

- Transition model:  
  \[
  P(S_t \mid S_{t-1}, \text{context}_t)
  \]
- Emission model:  
  \[
  P(\text{observations}_t \mid S_t)
  \]

Algorithms:

- **Baum–Welch** for parameter learning in HMMs.
- **Viterbi** for most likely state sequence.
- **Forward–backward** for \( P(S_t \mid \text{all observations}) \).

Use cases:

- Estimate probability she was in a “crisis” state on specific dates.
- Identify change-points where transition probabilities shift (e.g., postpartum week 6, med change).

***

### 2.3 Dynamic Bayesian Networks (DBNs)

Extend HMMs to multiple interacting variables:

- Nodes: sleep, meds, suicidality, cognition, childcare load, clinical contact, system fragmentation.
- Edges: causal or correlational dependencies over time.

Example:

\[
P(\text{suicidality}_t \mid \text{suicidality}_{t-1}, \text{sleep}_{t-1}, \text{meds}_{t-1}, \text{stress}_t)
\]

Algorithms:

- Structure learning (if you have many cases):
  - Score-based (BIC, BDeu) with search (greedy, hill-climbing).
  - Constraint-based (PC algorithm).
- Parameter learning:
  - Maximum likelihood or Bayesian estimation for conditional probability tables.
- Inference:
  - Belief propagation, variable elimination, or sampling.

Use:

- To quantify how much a missed follow-up or med change increases probability of crisis.
- To simulate “what-if” scenarios: what if a handoff had succeeded?

***

### 2.4 Survival Analysis / Time-to-Event Models

Model time to key events:

- Time from birth → first documented crisis.
- Time from first help-seeking → hospitalization.
- Time from last clinical contact → incident.

Models:

- **Cox proportional hazards**:  
  \[
  h(t \mid X) = h_0(t) \exp(\beta^\top X)
  \]
- **Parametric survival models** (Weibull, exponential).
- **Competing risks** if multiple possible endpoints (e.g., psych admission vs. medical admission vs. incident).

Inputs:

- Covariates: sleep, meds, number of children, work hours, prior mental health, system fragmentation metrics.

Outputs:

- Hazard ratios showing which factors most increase risk of catastrophic outcomes.
- Baseline hazard functions as priors for new cases.

***

### 2.5 Change-Point Detection

Identify moments where her trajectory fundamentally shifts.

Methods:

- **Bayesian change-point detection**: infer posterior over change-point locations.
- **CUSUM / EWMA** for simpler, online detection of shifts in mean/variance of signals (sleep, sentiment).
- **Hidden semi-Markov models** if you want explicit duration modeling for states.

Use:

- To mark “deterioration phases” objectively.
- To align system events (med changes, missed appointments) with detected change-points.

## 3) Using Data to Establish Priors

You want priors that reflect real postpartum populations and system behaviors, not just assumptions.

### 3.1 Prior Sources

- **Aggregate postpartum datasets** (if accessible):
  - Sleep patterns by week postpartum.
  - Prevalence of postpartum depression, anxiety, psychosis.
  - Treatment response rates.
- **Health system data** (de-identified, aggregated):
  - Follow-up adherence rates postpartum.
  - Handoff failure rates between OB ↔ psychiatry ↔ primary care.
- **Literature-derived rates**:
  - Meta-analyses for postpartum mental health trajectories.
  - Known risk factors (prior mental health, sleep deprivation, multiples, complications).

These become your **population-level priors** in HBMs and DBNs.

### 3.2 Prior Construction Patterns

For a parameter \( \theta \) (e.g., probability of crisis given severe sleep deprivation):

1. Start with a **Beta prior** if it’s a probability:
   \[
   \theta \sim \text{Beta}(\alpha, \beta)
   \]
   where \( \alpha, \beta \) come from aggregate counts (e.g., crises / non-crises under similar conditions).

2. Update with case-specific data:
   \[
   \theta \mid \text{data} \sim \text{Beta}(\alpha + k, \beta + n - k)
   \]
   where \( k \) = observed crises, \( n \) = total similar windows.

3. For multi-parameter models, use **weakly informative priors** (e.g., Normal(0, 2.5) on log-odds) to avoid overfitting with limited data.

## 4) Entity Resolution and Record Linkage

Before modeling, you need to be confident that records refer to the same entities (Lindsay, clinicians, visits).

Algorithms:

- **Probabilistic record linkage** (Fellegi–Sunter model):
  - Compute match probabilities based on name, DOB, address, phone, dates.
- **Bayesian entity resolution**:
  - Latent true entities; observed records are noisy manifestations.
  - Use MCMC or variational inference to infer posterior over linkages.

Why it matters:

- Mislinked records distort priors and posteriors.
- You can propagate linkage uncertainty into downstream models (e.g., integrate over possible linkages).

***

## 5) Narrative Uncertainty and “Competing Stories” as Models

You can treat prosecution/defense/media narratives as **competing generative models** of the same data.

For each narrative \( N_i \):

- Define a set of assumptions (e.g., “she was fully aware,” “she was psychotic,” “no one could have known”).
- Compute likelihood of observed data under each narrative:
  \[
  P(\text{data} \mid N_i)
  \]
- With priors \( P(N_i) \), get posterior:
  \[
  P(N_i \mid \text{data}) \propto P(\text{data} \mid N_i) P(N_i)
  \]

This doesn’t “pick a side”; it quantifies how well each story explains the evidence given the priors.

## 6) Practical Algorithm Stack (Python-Centric)

Given your preferences:

- **Probabilistic programming**:
  - PyMC (Bayesian models, HBMs, DBNs, change-point).
  - Stan (via PyStan) for robust MCMC.
- **Time-series / state-space**:
  - `statsmodels` (HMM-like via custom state-space).
  - `hmmlearn` for HMMs.
- **Survival analysis**:
  - `lifelines` (Cox, parametric models).
- **Graphical models**:
  - `pgmpy` (Bayesian networks, DBNs).
- **Entity resolution**:
  - `splink` (scalable probabilistic linkage).
  - Custom PyMC models for Bayesian entity resolution.

Integration pattern:

1. Ingest → clean → link entities (splink / custom).
2. Build event table in Supabase.
3. Export aggregates to Python for model fitting.
4. Store model parameters and key posterior summaries back in Supabase for UI consumption.

## 7) Example: A Minimal V1 Model

For V1, you might implement:

1. **Latent risk state HMM**:
   - States: stable, distressed, crisis.
   - Observations: sleep, journal sentiment, med changes, clinical contacts.
   - Output: \( P(\text{crisis}_t) \) for each day.

2. **System fragmentation score**:
   - Simple Bayesian model:
     - Prior: base rate of missed handoffs from literature/aggregates.
     - Data: count of missed follow-ups, unshared records, contradictory instructions.
     - Output: \( P(\text{high fragmentation} \mid \text{case data}) \).

3. **Change-point detection** on:
   - Sleep + sentiment + suicidality tags.
   - Output: dates where deterioration accelerates.

These three together give you:

- A probabilistic deterioration curve.
- A system-failure probability.
- Objective markers of key turning points.

***

## 8) Guardrails for Ethical Use of Probabilities

- Never present probabilities as verdicts; always as **contextual risk estimates**.
- Show **credible intervals**, not just point estimates.
- Make priors explicit and auditable: “This prior comes from X dataset / Y study.”
- Allow users to adjust priors (e.g., “assume higher base-rate of system failure”) and see how posteriors change.
