It lets you demonstrate the core capability using material already available to researchers and creators, without beginning inside healthcare, law enforcement, legal evidence, or HIPAA-regulated workflows.

The first product is:

> **A case-reconstruction workspace that turns scattered source material into an attributable claim graph, verified timeline, evidence map, and supportable narrative.**

### The research problem

True-crime creators currently work across:

* police reports and affidavits
* court filings and transcripts
* interviews and testimony
* 911 calls and body-camera footage
* medical information made public in proceedings
* texts, call logs, calendars, and location records
* news reporting
* podcasts and documentaries
* social posts and public statements

They manually create timelines in spreadsheets or documents. Reported claims, verified facts, repeated allegations, and creator interpretations gradually blend together.

Icarus preserves the distinctions.

### The product’s central question

It would not promise:

> “Here is what really happened.”

It would answer:

> **What was claimed, by whom, based on what, when did it enter the record, what independently supports it, what contradicts it, and which reconstruction best fits the available evidence?**

That is already transformative.

### What users would see

For every material assertion:

| Layer                | Example                                                      |
| -------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Original source      | Police report, page 18                                       |
| Attributed claim     | Husband said she was asleep when he left                     |
| Event described      | Woman reportedly asleep at approximately 6:10 p.m.           |
| Independent evidence | Phone activity, camera footage, witness observation          |
| Status               | Reported / supported / contested / contradicted / unresolved |
| Claim lineage        | Husband → police → news → podcast                            |
| Narrative use        | Included with qualification and citation                     |

A creator could immediately see that six publications repeating one statement still trace back to one originating source.

### The killer feature

The strongest initial experience is probably:

> **Upload everything about a case and receive a reconstructed, source-linked timeline with claims separated from evidence.**

Then users can toggle:

* reported account
* independently timestamped activity
* physical evidence
* location and communications
* medical history contained in the public record
* witness testimony
* contradictions
* unknown intervals
* competing reconstructions
* behavioral changes over weeks, months, or years

Clicking any timeline point opens the exact page, quotation, recording timestamp, or data row supporting it.

### Longitudinal pattern-of-life becomes the differentiator

Most research tools could summarize a police report. Icarus could reconstruct how the case evolved:

* What was the person’s baseline?
* What changed?
* Who observed the change?
* Were those observations independent?
* Did sleep, language, movement, relationships, healthcare, or routines change?
* Did repeated conflicts produce the same outcome?
* Which later claims are consistent with contemporaneous records?
* Which popular “facts” entered the story only after the incident?
* How did the official narrative change over time?

That gives creators something far better than a dossier. It gives them the **structure of the case**.

### The minimum viable product

Start with six capabilities:

1. **Case room**
   Upload PDFs, transcripts, audio, video, spreadsheets, screenshots, and links.

2. **Source reader**
   Search everything while retaining page- and timestamp-level citations.

3. **Claim extraction**
   Convert statements into attributed claims without promoting them to facts.

4. **Entity resolution**
   Connect aliases, people, devices, phone numbers, addresses, and organizations.

5. **Layered timeline**
   Compare testimony and reported accounts with timestamps, calls, locations, and other records.

6. **Cited story builder**
   Draft a video script, article, episode outline, or evidence brief where each assertion links back to its source.

### Keep the first boundary clean

The research version should explicitly avoid:

* guilt or innocence scores
* psychiatric diagnoses
* “narcissist detection”
* automated credibility verdicts
* claims that evidence is court-admissible
* private medical-record collection
* direct law-enforcement integrations
* clinical recommendations

It reconstructs the available record and exposes its epistemic structure.

A creator can say:

> “This event is supported by three independent records.”

Or:

> “This claim appears 14 times online, but every version originates with the husband’s first statement.”

That alone could materially improve true-crime reporting.

### Why this is the right demonstration environment

True-crime cases are difficult enough to prove what Icarus can do:

* multiple people with conflicting accounts
* aliases and shifting descriptions
* enormous document collections
* uncertain and approximate times
* repeated claims mistaken for corroboration
* evidence scattered across formats
* critical gaps
* emotionally powerful narratives
* behavior that only becomes meaningful longitudinally

And the output is naturally demonstrable. You could take one well-documented public case and show:

**Before Icarus:** 3,000 pages, 40 hours of media, dozens of accounts, one dominant narrative.

**After Icarus:** every claim attributed, every source connected, every contradiction surfaced, and every supported reconstruction reproducible.

Then, after proving that the architecture works, the higher-stakes versions become possible:

* investigative journalism
* innocence projects and defense research
* forensic psychiatric reconstruction
* law-enforcement case analysis
* perinatal-health longitudinal records
* multi-informant clinical monitoring
* authenticated legal evidence systems

Those later versions would require their own validation, governance, security, consent, and compliance. But the core reasoning object remains identical:

[
\text{Sources}
\rightarrow
\text{Claims}
\rightarrow
\text{Events}
\rightarrow
\text{Evidence}
\rightarrow
\text{Sequences}
\rightarrow
\text{Competing hypotheses}
\rightarrow
\text{Supportable narrative}
]

The clean positioning is:

> **Icarus Casework reconstructs true-crime cases from the source record—showing what was said, what the evidence supports, where accounts conflict, and what remains unknown.**

Or more pointedly:

> **Every claim has a source. Every timeline has gaps. See the case before the story swallowed it.**
