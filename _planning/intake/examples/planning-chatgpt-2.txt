The “specific sailor knot” claim must remain **unverified** until it can be tied to testimony, photographs, an exhibit description, or qualified knot analysis. Reliable public reporting I found confirms exercise-band ligatures, but does not yet establish the precise knot characterization.

If the configuration matters, the research process would be:

1. Obtain the best legally public images or exhibit descriptions.
2. Have the structure classified without telling the examiner the preferred theory.
3. Determine whether it is genuinely specialized.
4. Estimate how quickly novices and experienced people can reproduce it.
5. Search the record for demonstrated knot experience, occupation, hobbies, instructional searches, or prior exposure for every person with opportunity.

“Women don’t know sailor knots” cannot serve as a model input. A defensible variable is:

> **Does this individual have demonstrated knowledge or exposure sufficient to construct this specific configuration under the available conditions?**

Gender can inform a carefully sourced population prior, but individual evidence must dominate it.

## How the gender and method research belongs

The relevant research needs a carefully defined reference class:

* neonaticide versus infanticide versus filicide
* one victim versus multiple children
* maternal versus paternal filicide
* filicide-suicide or attempted suicide
* psychotic versus nonpsychotic perpetrator
* biological parent versus stepparent
* strangulation versus suffocation versus ligature strangulation
* familicide involving a spouse versus children alone
* victim ages
* planned versus impulsive methods

These cannot be mixed casually.

Existing literature does report sex-linked differences at the group level. Reviews describe maternal filicide methods as including drowning, suffocation, strangulation, and head trauma, while fathers more consistently use active violent methods and are more likely to kill or attempt to kill a spouse as well. Another study found suffocation/smothering more frequent among mothers and more violent methods among fathers. [Filicide overview](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2922347/), [filicide and mental illness study](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3617183/)

But the exact proposition you want is narrower:

> **How frequently do female filicide perpetrators use separately prepared ligatures with deliberately maintained pressure, and how often is that method associated with particular diagnoses, planning, multiple victims, offender sex, or a second participant?**

That may require building a new coded dataset from published cases because existing category labels often collapse hand strangulation, ligature strangulation, and suffocation together.

Your “objects close at hand” idea can become a testable variable:

[
\text{method preparation distance}
==================================

\text{planning}
+
\text{specialized knowledge}
+
\text{material acquisition}
+
\text{departure from routine caregiving context}
]

Drowning during bathing, smothering with bedding, manual strangulation, and constructing a maintained ligature should not be treated as equivalent simply because all involve asphyxia. The preparation, affordances, physical interaction, and required skill may differ materially.

## The actual simulation

For each competing proposition, run three stages.

### Stage A: Hard feasibility

Reject sequences that violate:

* authenticated timestamps
* minimum travel time
* physical location
* action duration
* medical constraints
* necessary precursors
* known access limitations

### Stage B: Monte Carlo uncertainty

Sample uncertain values:

* departure and return windows
* clock error
* action durations
* movement time
* injury-time estimates
* medication exposure
* sleep estimates
* witness-time error
* knot-construction time
* required recovery or transition time

### Stage C: Bayesian evidence comparison

Ask whether each observation is more expected under different propositions:

* deliberate conduct with intact criminal responsibility
* conduct during psychosis or severe mood disorder
* another specified impaired mental state
* incomplete or inaccurate event reconstruction
* third-party involvement, only if actual evidence justifies testing it

The model would never convert population method statistics into “a woman would not do this.” It would report something like:

> “This method appears uncommon in the selected maternal-filicide reference dataset. Its evidentiary value remains limited because the dataset contains only N comparable cases, method coding is inconsistent, and individual skill evidence is incomplete.”

## The V1 output

The strongest deliverable would be an interactive reconstruction with five views:

1. **What the jury has heard**
2. **What the underlying sources establish**
3. **Lindsay’s 90-day state trajectory**
4. **The care and household operating environment**
5. **Conditions required by each competing reconstruction**

The scenario report might say:

> The prosecution reconstruction requires the incident sequence to fit within X–Y minutes, assumes Lindsay independently constructed three ligatures, and relies on these indicators of planning and concealment.

> The defense reconstruction requires severe impairment at the incident time despite apparently organized behaviors, and relies on this medication, sleep, symptom, hospitalization, and post-incident evidence.

> The knot-origin hypothesis remains unresolved because the public record currently lacks adequate classification, construction-time testing, and individual skill evidence.

> The household record shows that childcare coordination remained substantially assigned to Lindsay despite documented deterioration; the significance of that fact is interpretive and requires comparison with testimony concerning support offers and perceived functioning.

That is an extraordinary V1 because it does not begin with “Lindsay is innocent,” “Patrick did it,” or “postpartum psychosis explains everything.”

It begins with:

> **Here is every proposition. Here is its source. Here is the sequence. Here are the conditions each story requires. Here is where the evidence supports, strains, or fails to distinguish among them.**
