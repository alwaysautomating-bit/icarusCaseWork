icarus Casework v1 : reconstruction engine for True Crime podcast research

### 1) Purpose and Scope

Icarus v1 is a reconstruction engine that assembles a multi-source, time-indexed narrative of a person’s deterioration and the surrounding system failures, using their own records plus institutional fragments—without allowing any single actor or institution to become the default narrator. 

For the Lindsay Clancy case, the goal is not to argue guilt/innocence or identify an alternative perpetrator, but to answer: **What had to happen to this woman—in her body, brain, household, and healthcare system—for January 24 to become possible?** 

This becomes a reusable framework for other postpartum cases where suffering becomes a tragedy, a diagnosis attached only to them, or a criminal case.

***

### 2) Core Principles

These principles protect the work from advocacy drift and narrative capture:

- **Preserve her voice.** Her dated words, messages, and journal entries are primary. 
- **Reconstruct the conditions.** Model sleep, meds, clinical contacts, workload, childcare, and household demands as first-class data. 
- **Test every account equally.** Police, clinicians, family, media, prosecution, defense—all are treated as partial, time-stamped observations. 
- **Show evidence and uncertainty.** Every claim is traceable to a source, with explicit confidence/uncertainty tags. 
- **No default narrator.** The system, not a person or institution, is the narrator; all attributions are explicit. 

***

### 3) Narrative Structure (V1 Story Arc)

The reconstruction is organized into five sections, each driven by time-indexed evidence.

#### 3.1 The Woman

Baseline reconstruction:

- Identity and roles: labor-and-delivery nurse, partner, parent of three young children. 
- Work patterns, shifts, on-call expectations, commute, and support network. 
- Pregnancy, birth, postpartum physiology, prior mental health history, and functioning before deterioration. 

Outputs:

- A “baseline profile” with timelines of work, caregiving load, and known health status.

#### 3.2 The Deterioration

A layered timeline built from her own words plus parallel streams:

- **Her journal and communications**: dated entries, texts, emails, posts. 
- **Sleep**: documented or inferred sleep duration/quality. 
- **Medications**: prescriptions, changes, adherence, side effects. 
- **Clinical contacts**: OB, psychiatry, primary care, ER visits, telehealth. 
- **Weight/appetite/cognition**: self-reports, clinical notes. 
- **Suicidality and ideation**: explicit statements, plans, urges. 
- **Hospital admissions**: psych holds, medical admissions, discharge plans. 
- **Observations from others**: partner, family, friends, coworkers, clinicians. 
- **Childcare and household demands**: who did what, when, and under what constraints. 

Outputs:

- A **multi-stream deterioration timeline** with confidence scores and source attributions for each event.

#### 3.3 The System Around Her

Focus on information flow and responsibility:

- **Who knew what, when?** Map each clinician’s view of the record at each point. 
- **Fragmentation**: what information failed to move between providers, settings, or caregivers? 
- **Support offered vs. responsibility retained**: referrals, safety plans, follow-ups, and what remained her burden. 
- **Institutional constraints**: visit lengths, documentation burdens, handoff practices, insurance/coverage limits. 

Outputs:

- A **system graph** showing nodes (clinicians, institutions, family) and edges (information shared, referrals, missed handoffs).

#### 3.4 The Incident Window

A constrained, evidence-only reconstruction of the final catastrophic interval:

- **Timestamps**: calls, texts, device activity, location pings, known movements. 
- **Physical evidence**: scene reports, medical examiner findings, photos, 911 logs. 
- **Medical evidence**: vitals, injuries, intubation, core temperature (~82.1°F), thoracic-spine fractures, paralysis, communication via whiteboard while intubated. 
- **Explicit uncertainty**: mark unknowns, conflicting accounts, and gaps; do not fill with speculation. 

Outputs:

- A **minute-by-minute (or best-available) incident timeline** with confidence levels and “unknown” markers.

#### 3.5 The Narrative Capture

Track how her story became public narrative:

- **Attributed statements → police assumptions**: which quotes or behaviors became “facts” in the investigation? 
- **Media facts**: how headlines and stories hardened partial accounts into settled narrative. 
- **Prosecution/defense theories**: how each side framed intent, responsibility, and mental state. 
- **Public beliefs**: dominant narratives about postpartum women, “monsters,” and maternal fitness. 

Outputs:

- A **narrative genealogy** showing how specific claims traveled and mutated across actors and time.

#### 3.6 The Postpartum Story (Synthesis)

Use the reconstruction to articulate what her case reveals:

- Treatment of maternal distress as an individual defect vs. a system-level failure. 
- Unmeasured burdens: care load, sleep deprivation, relationship strain, treatment fragmentation, operating environment. 
- Design implications for clinical, legal, and social systems to recognize at-risk postpartum women earlier.

### 4) Data Model (V1)

Icarus treats everything as time-indexed, source-attributed events.

#### 4.1 Core Entities

- **Person**: Lindsay (primary subject), plus other actors (partner, clinicians, family, institutions).
- **Event**: Any time-stamped occurrence (journal entry, med change, call, visit, observation).
- **Document**: Source artifact (journal page, medical record, text thread, trial transcript, news article).
- **Claim**: A derived statement (e.g., “She reported suicidal ideation on X date”) with:
  - source_refs: list of document IDs
  - confidence: low/medium/high or numeric
  - uncertainty_notes: what is unclear or contested
- **SystemNode**: Clinician, clinic, hospital, insurer, family member, etc.
- **InformationFlow**: Directed edge showing what information moved between nodes and when.

#### 4.2 Event Schema (Simplified)

Each event includes:

- `id`
- `timestamp` (or range)
- `type` (journal_entry, med_change, clinical_visit, call, observation, etc.)
- `actor` (who generated or is described)
- `content` (text, summary, or structured fields)
- `source_documents` (IDs)
- `confidence`
- `uncertainty_flags`
- `tags` (sleep, meds, suicidality, cognition, childcare, etc.)

### 5) Ingestion and Evidence Pipeline

#### 5.1 Source Types (V1)

For the Lindsay case, prioritize:

- **Her journal and personal writings** (as reported in media and trial coverage). 
- **Medical records**: OB, psychiatry, ER, hospitalizations, discharge summaries. 
- **Communications**: texts, emails, voicemails referenced in reporting or court. 
- **Witness testimony**: partner, family, coworkers, clinicians (via trial transcripts, news). 
- **Trial record**: prosecution/defense filings, expert testimony, judge’s instructions, verdict/coverage. 
- **Media coverage**: AP, local news, long-form features that cite documents or testimony. 

#### 5.2 Ingestion Steps

1. **Collect and normalize** documents into a canonical format (PDF/text/structured).
2. **Extract time-indexed events** using:
   - Deterministic rules (dates, times, med names, dosages, visit types).
   - LLM-assisted extraction with strict schema and human review for sensitive claims.
3. **Link events to source documents** with page/section references.
4. **Tag events** by domain (sleep, meds, suicidality, cognition, childcare, system failures).
5. **Assign confidence and uncertainty** based on:
   - Directness (her own words vs. secondhand report).
   - Corroboration (multiple independent sources).
   - Consistency (internal and cross-source).

### 6) Reconstruction Engine

#### 6.1 Timeline Construction

- Build a **unified chronological stream** of events across all domains.
- Allow multiple “views”:
  - Her-voice view (her entries and communications prioritized).
  - Clinical view (medical/psych events).
  - Household view (childcare, sleep, domestic load).
  - System view (information flows and missed handoffs).
  - Incident view (final interval only).

#### 6.2 Uncertainty Handling

- Every claim has:
  - `confidence_level`
  - `conflicting_accounts` (if any)
  - `unknowns` (explicit gaps)
- Visual cues in UI: solid lines (high confidence), dashed (medium), dotted (low), gray gaps (unknown).

#### 6.3 Narrative Capture Module

- Track how specific claims evolve:
  - Original statement → police report → indictment → media headline → public narrative.
- Tag transformations:
  - Omission, amplification, re-framing, decontextualization.

***

### 7) Ethical and Legal Guardrails

These are non-negotiable design constraints.

- **No advocacy mode.** The system does not start from “save Lindsay,” “convict Lindsay,” or “prove Patrick did it.” 
- **Human-in-the-loop for sensitive inferences.** Any mental-state or intent-related inference must be reviewable and editable by a human researcher.
- **Privacy and dignity.** Even in a public case, minimize gratuitous detail; focus on structural understanding.
- **Clear provenance.** Every displayed fact must be traceable to a source; no hidden AI inference presented as fact.
- **Uncertainty-first framing.** When evidence is weak or conflicting, the UI must foreground that, not bury it.

### 8) User Experience (V1)

Target users: researchers, journalists, advocates, clinicians, policy designers.

#### 8.1 Core Views

- **Timeline Explorer**: scrollable, filterable by domain (sleep, meds, clinical, household, system).
- **System Map**: graph of actors and information flows, with highlighted breaks/missed handoffs.
- **Incident Reconstructor**: focused view on the final interval, with explicit unknowns.
- **Narrative Genealogy**: flow diagram of how specific claims traveled and mutated.
- **Evidence Panel**: for any selected event/claim, show source excerpts, confidence, and notes.

#### 8.2 Interaction Patterns

- Click any claim → see sources, confidence, and conflicting accounts.
- Toggle filters: “Show only Lindsay’s own words,” “Show only clinical events,” etc.
- Export: generate a report for a specific question (e.g., “What did clinicians know by date X?”).

### 10) V1 Scope for the Lindsay Case

To keep V1 achievable:

- **In scope**:
  - Journal and key personal writings (as available via reporting). 
  - Major medical/psychiatric contacts and hospitalizations. 
  - Key witness observations cited in trial/media. 
  - Core incident-window facts from trial reporting and AP accounts. 
  - Narrative capture for 5–10 pivotal claims (e.g., “she was fine,” “she planned this,” “no one knew”). 

- **Out of scope (for V1)**:
  - Full trial transcript ingestion.
  - Exhaustive media corpus.
  - Deep forensic reconstruction beyond what’s publicly documented.

***

### 11) Success Criteria (V1)

Icarus v1 succeeds if it can:

- Produce a **coherent, source-backed timeline** of Lindsay’s deterioration and system context. 
- Make her **own voice clearly audible** alongside institutional fragments. 
- Surface **specific, concrete system failures** (missed handoffs, fragmented care, unshared information). 
- Represent the **incident window** with disciplined uncertainty, not speculation. 
- Show how **narratives formed and hardened** from partial accounts. 
- Be adaptable as a template for other postpartum cases without re-architecting.
