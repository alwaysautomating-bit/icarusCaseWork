The most important thing this reconstruction exposes is that there are actually several concurrent lanes, not one simple chain:

**Police / Patrick lane**

`Police arrive → Lindsay outside → Patrick goes inside → dispatch: can't wake them → scream → Hall/Josephine enter → RIGHT basement → Patrick → Dawson`

**Fire/EMS arrival lane**

`Fire arrives while police are outside → Lindsay treatment begins → Ambulance 1 repositioning → additional fire personnel enter`

**Dawson lane**

`Dawson discovered RIGHT → Josephine removes Dawson → front door → ambulance arriving/backing in → CPR/advanced care`

**Cora/Callan lane**

`Fire/EMS redirected inside → LEFT basement → Cora + Callan → CPR → Hall/Josephine return from Dawson → find treatment already underway`

That concurrency is important. Dwyer's testimony says that when he and Stratton reach the basement LEFT, Patrick is sitting on a couch near the two children and they begin CPR. Meanwhile, Hall/Josephine have a separate RIGHT-side encounter with Patrick and Dawson before removing Dawson and subsequently returning LEFT. So Patrick's movement through the basement itself becomes another reconstructable lane.

There is also an excellent external visual anchor from Hussey. While he is outside supervising Lindsay's treatment, he hears a scream from the basement, looks through the basement window, and sees Patrick with a child, “unwrapping something from a child's head.” That potentially synchronizes the **outside EMS clock** with the **Patrick/Dawson basement clock** without relying on anybody's estimated minutes.

The biggest caution is the clock. We should **not** currently label things like “6:24” as established fact just because estimated travel times produce that number. Josephine says 3–4 minutes from a \~6:11 dispatch; Hall says 7–10 minutes but also says he arrived simultaneously with Josephine. Those recollected durations cannot all be literally exact.

For Icarus, I would therefore model this first as a **partial-order reconstruction**:

`DISPATCH`
→ `POLICE ARRIVAL`
→ `PATRICK ENTERS HOUSE`
→ `RADIO: CAN'T WAKE THEM`
→ `SCREAM`
→ `POLICE ENTER`
→ `RIGHT BASEMENT / PATRICK`
→ `DAWSON OBSERVED`
→ `DAWSON REMOVED`
→ `DAWSON ↔ AMBULANCE ARRIVAL`
→ `POLICE RETURN LEFT`

with a parallel branch:

`FIRE ARRIVAL`
→ `LINDSAY EMS`
→ `EMS REDIRECTED INSIDE`
→ `LEFT BASEMENT`
→ `CORA + CALLAN CPR`

and then use CAD, dispatch audio, ambulance timestamps, 911 audio, bodycam/device clocks, etc. to progressively pin those ordered nodes to actual seconds.

That is considerably more useful than a conventional timeline because we already know **what must precede what even where the clock time remains unresolved**. And the testimony gives us several synchronization events—especially **the scream**, **Dawson reaching Ambulance 1 as it backs in**, and **Hall/Josephine returning LEFT after Dawson and finding CPR already underway**—that can eventually collapse this into a seconds-level reconstruction.
